How the Sumbreak solver works

The solver tries merges the same way you would, but much faster. It starts with the smallest numbers, because those are the tiles most likely to get stranded, and skips any position that cannot be finished – for example when a dead spot splits the board in two, or when an odd number of the smallest tile is left. As soon as it finds an order of merges that ends in one tile, it shows you the full path.

Wildcard tiles make the search harder, because a ×2 or +3 can join any number and therefore opens many more possible moves. The solver handles this by checking the numbers first: if the smallest number on the board has neither an identical partner nor a wildcard left to use, that branch is dropped straight away. It also spots tiles that can never move again – for example a 4 that is surrounded only by bigger numbers and empty squares. The search runs in the background of your browser, so the page stays responsive even on 6×6 boards.

Worked example: a 3×3 board in eight merges

Here is a small board we made for this page: 2 2 4 / 8 16 4 / 8 16 4. Its numbers add up to 64, a power of two, so a full solution is possible. The solver's answer shows why order matters:

  1. Merge the two 2s in the top row into the middle square: now · 4 4.
  2. Merge that 4 into the top-right 4, making 8 in the corner.
  3. Merge the bottom-right 4 up into the 4 in the middle of the right column, making 8 there.
  4. Drop the corner 8 onto that 8: the right column now holds a single 16 in its middle square.
  5. Merge the two 8s in the left column, making 16 at the bottom left.
  6. Merge the right-hand 16 into the centre 16, making 32 in the centre.
  7. Merge the bottom-left 16 into the bottom-middle 16, making 32 below the centre.
  8. Merge the centre 32 down onto it: one tile, 64. Solved.

Notice that the solver starts with the smallest tiles and keeps every new number next to a partner. Merge the two 16s in the middle column first instead and the board can no longer be finished – the 32 is left without a partner it can ever reach, and the solver will tell you there is no solution. FAQ

How do I get today's Sumbreak answer?

Open today's puzzle on LinkedIn, choose the matching board size here, type each number into the same position (leave dead spots blank) and press Solve. You will get the full list of merges in order.

Why does the solver say there is no solution?

Usually one number was typed in the wrong cell or a wildcard is missing. On boards without wildcards, all the numbers add up to a power of two such as 64, 128, 256 or 512 – a quick way to check your typing.

Does the solver handle the ×2 and +3 wildcard tiles?

Yes. Type a wildcard as x2, ×2 or +3 (or use the wildcard buttons under the grid). The solver knows that a wildcard can merge with any neighbouring number and that two wildcards cannot merge with each other.

What board sizes are supported?

Every size from 3×3 up to 6×6, including rectangular boards such as 4×5 and 5×6. Leave empty or dead spots blank.

Can I see just one hint instead of the whole answer?

Yes. After you press Solve, only the first merge is shown. Press Show next step to reveal one more merge at a time, or Show all steps for the full answer.

Does the solver work for unlimited puzzles too?

Yes. It works for any board that follows the merge rules: identical numbers that touch up, down, left or right combine into their sum, and wildcards change any number they touch.

Is using the solver cheating?

That is up to you. Many players only reveal the first step or two, then finish the puzzle themselves. If you want practice instead, play our unlimited boards.
