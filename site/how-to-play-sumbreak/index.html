How to Play Sumbreak: Rules, Tips and Strategy

Everything you need to solve Sumbreak-style merge puzzles – from the basic rules to the tricks that stop you getting stuck.

▶ Practise now – unlimited puzzles

What is Sumbreak?

Sumbreak is a daily math-and-logic puzzle that launched on LinkedIn on 18 November 2026. You start with a grid of numbered tiles. Merging two identical neighbours adds them together, and you win when only one tile is left. It feels a little like 2048, but there is no sliding and no luck: every move is your choice, and one careless merge can leave a number with no partner.

The rules

  1. You may merge two tiles only if they show the same number.
  2. The two tiles must be orthogonal neighbours – sharing a side, not just a corner.
  3. Drag one tile onto the other. The target becomes the sum; the tile you dragged becomes an empty dead spot.
  4. Dead spots stay empty for the rest of the puzzle. Tiles never slide or fall.
  5. The puzzle is solved when exactly one tile remains.

Strategy guide

1. Start with the smallest numbers

No move ever creates a small number – merges only make bigger ones. So if a 1 or a 2 is left without a partner, the puzzle is lost. Pair up the smallest tiles early while they still have neighbours.

2. Count before you merge

The smallest value on the board must always appear an even number of times (unless only one tile is left). If you count three 2s and no 1s, one of those 2s can never be paired – undo and try another route.

3. Don't split the board

Every merge leaves a dead spot. If the dead spots form a wall that cuts the tiles into two separate groups, those groups can never join again. Before merging, ask: will the tile I'm moving away from still be connected?

4. Choose which tile moves

Merging A onto B is not the same as merging B onto A. The result stays where you drop it, and the empty spot appears where you started. Drop the result next to the number it needs to meet next, and move the tile that sits on the edge or in a corner.

5. Work towards the big tile

Large numbers (like 64 or 256) usually can't move until late in the puzzle. Build matching numbers next to them, step by step, instead of dragging small tiles far away from the big ones.

6. Check the total

Add up all the numbers on the board. The final tile will be that total, and it is always a power of two. This also helps you check that you copied a board correctly into the solver.

Worked example

Take the board 1 1 64 / 4 2 32 / 4 4 16 (total 128). First merge the two 1s into a 2 – it now sits next to the other 2. Merge those into a 4, then pair up the 4s into 8s and 16s, building up to 32 and 64 until a single 128 remains. Try it yourself in the solver using Load example.

FAQ

Can tiles merge diagonally in Sumbreak?

No. Only tiles that share a side – up, down, left or right – can be merged.

Can you merge two different numbers?

No. Only identical numbers combine. A 4 can merge with a 4 to make 8, but never with a 2 or an 8.

What happens to the tile you drag?

It disappears and leaves an empty dead spot. The sum appears where you dropped it.

What is the final number in Sumbreak?

It is the total of all numbers on the starting board, which is always a power of two (for example 128 in the first puzzle).

When does Sumbreak reset?

The official LinkedIn puzzle changes every day at midnight Pacific Time. Our unlimited boards never run out.
