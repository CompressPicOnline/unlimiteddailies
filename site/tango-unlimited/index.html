How to play Tango

  1. Fill every square. Tap a square once for a sun , twice for a moon , and a third time to clear it. Squares with a grey background are given and cannot be changed.
  2. Balance each line. Every row and every column needs exactly as many suns as moons – three of each on a 6×6 board, four of each on the 8×8 Expert board.
  3. Never three in a row. Two identical symbols may sit side by side, but a third one next to them, horizontally or vertically, breaks the rule.
  4. Follow the signs. An = between two squares means they hold the same symbol; a × means they hold opposite symbols.
  5. Finish the grid. Anything that breaks a rule is striped in red at once, and the puzzle is solved the moment the last square is filled correctly.

Tango strategy: six patterns that solve most boards

1. Close off every pair

Whenever two identical symbols sit side by side, the squares at both ends of the pair must be the opposite symbol. Two suns together? Put a moon on each side. This is the single most common move in the game.

2. Fill the gap

If two identical symbols have exactly one empty square between them – sun, gap, sun – the gap must be the opposite symbol, otherwise you would get three suns in a row.

3. Complete a full half

On a 6×6 board, once a row has three suns, every remaining square in that row is a moon (and the other way round). Count after every move: this rule often finishes a line in one go.

4. Chain the signs

Signs pass information along. If A = B and B × C, then A and C are opposite. A run of signs across a row can fix three or four squares as soon as one of them is known.

5. Two moons left, three gaps

Near the end of a line, look at which arrangements are still possible. If a row needs two more moons and one sun in three empty squares, and the sun cannot go in the middle without creating a triple, the options shrink fast. Checking the few possible arrangements of one line is exactly how our hint finds its next square.

6. Switch between rows and columns

Every square belongs to one row and one column. When a row stops giving you anything, look at the column of the square you just filled – each new symbol is a new clue in the other direction.

Common Tango mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  • Reading × as "empty". The × sign never means "leave blank" – every square gets a symbol. It only says the two squares differ.
  • Checking only rows. A row can look perfect while its columns already hold four suns. After each symbol, glance down its column as well as across its row.
  • Forgetting the gap rule. Most players spot pairs at once but miss sun–gap–sun. Scan for one-square gaps between equal symbols on every pass.
  • Guessing on Hard boards. Every board here can be solved without guessing. If you feel stuck, look for the line with the fewest empty squares and list its possible arrangements – usually only one or two remain.
  • Racing the clock too early. Speed comes from recognising patterns, not from tapping faster. Solve Easy and Medium boards cleanly first, then chase times on the daily.

Worked example: one row, one sign

Imagine a 6×6 row where the first two squares already hold suns, the sixth square holds a moon, and there is an = sign between the third and fourth squares. Because of the pair, square 3 must be a moon. The = sign then makes square 4 a moon too. The row now has two suns and three moons, so the last empty square, square 5, must be the third sun – giving sun, sun, moon, moon, sun, moon, with no triple anywhere. A sun, a pair and one sign filled three squares without any guessing – and each of them now gives you a new clue in its column.

What are the rules of Tango?

Fill every square with a sun or a moon. Each row and each column must contain the same number of suns and moons (three of each on a 6×6 board). No more than two identical symbols may be next to each other in a row or column. Two squares joined by = must be the same, and two squares joined by × must be opposite.

What does the × sign mean in Tango?

× means the two squares it sits between must hold different symbols: one sun and one moon. It does not tell you which one goes where – you find that out from the rest of the row or column.

Easy, Medium and Hard are 6×6 boards like the daily puzzle on LinkedIn: Easy starts with many symbols filled in, Medium with fewer, and Hard with only the minimum needed. Expert is a bigger 8×8 board with four suns and four moons per line.

Can I play with a keyboard?

How is Tango different from Queens and Sumbreak?

Tango is about balance: two symbols, equal counts per line and = / × signs between squares. Queens is about placement: one queen per row, column and colour region, with no two queens touching. Sumbreak is a number puzzle where you merge equal neighbours until one tile is left. All three are logic puzzles you can play free and unlimited here.
