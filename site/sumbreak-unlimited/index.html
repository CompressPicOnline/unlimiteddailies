How to play Sumbreak Unlimited

  1. Find a pair. Look for two identical numbers that touch side by side or above and below each other. Diagonal tiles do not count.
  2. Merge them. Drag one number onto the other (or tap one, then tap the other). They combine into their sum: 4 + 4 becomes 8.
  3. Watch the dead spot. The tile you moved leaves an empty space behind. Empty spaces can never be filled again, so they can cut the board into pieces.
  4. Use wildcards wisely. Purple ×2 and +N tiles merge with any neighbouring number: ×2 doubles it, +3 adds three. Medium, Hard and Expert boards include them, just like the wildcard tiles in the newer official puzzles.
  5. Finish with one tile. The puzzle is solved when a single number remains. Every board here is guaranteed to have a solution.

Quick tips

  • Clear the smallest numbers first. A 1 or 2 that ends up alone can never be merged, because no new small numbers are ever created.
  • Count each value: an odd number of the smallest tile on the board means something has already gone wrong.
  • Before each merge, check that you are not cutting the board in two with a new dead spot.
  • Merge so the result lands next to a matching number, keeping chains going.
  • Treat a wildcard as a rescue tool: look for the one number on the board that has no possible partner (an odd one like 13, or a lonely 8 surrounded by bigger tiles) – that is usually where the wildcard belongs.
  • Remember that two wildcards never merge with each other, so don't let them end up side by side with no number around them.

Worked example: using a ×2 wildcard

Imagine a corner that reads 4 4 / ×2 16. Merging the two 4s first gives 8, but then the ×2 has to double that 8 into 16 to meet the 16 – so the order matters: merge the right-hand 4 onto the left-hand 4 so the new 8 sits directly above the wildcard, then drag that 8 onto the ×2. The 16 lands where the wildcard was – right next to the other 16 – and the last merge makes 32. If you had used the ×2 on one of the 4s first, you would be left with an 8 and a 4 that can never meet. Wildcards are powerful, but only at the right moment.

How do you play Sumbreak?

Drag a number onto an identical number right next to it (up, down, left or right). The two tiles combine into their sum, and the tile you moved leaves an empty dead spot. Keep merging until only one number is left on the board.

What is the difference between Easy, Medium, Hard and Expert?

Easy is a 3×3 board with plain numbers. Medium is 4×4 with one wildcard tile, Hard is 5×5 with two wildcards, and Expert is a big 6×6 board with 36 tiles and two wildcards.

What are the ×2 and +3 tiles?

They are wildcard tiles. A wildcard can merge with any number next to it, not just an identical one: ×2 doubles the number and +3 adds three to it. The result is a normal number that then has to find its own identical partner.

Where should I start on a Sumbreak board?

Start with the smallest numbers, especially any that sit on an edge or in a corner. They have the fewest neighbours, so they are the first tiles to get stranded. Then check that the merge you pick drops the result next to the number it has to meet next.

Why won't two numbers merge?

Two plain numbers merge only when they are exactly the same and share a side. A 4 next to an 8 cannot merge, and neither can two 4s that only touch at a corner. If one of the tiles is a wildcard (×2 or +N) it can merge with any number it touches, but two wildcards can never merge with each other.

Can I play past daily puzzles?

Yes. Every daily puzzle we have published is kept in the daily archive on our home page. Pick any number to replay it; solved ones get a tick. Archive games count towards your stats, but only today's puzzle extends your streak.

Does using a hint or undo affect my streak?

No. Hints and undo are there to help you learn, so they never reset your streak or your stats. Your best time is simply the time on the clock when you finish.

Can I play with a keyboard?

Yes. Press Tab to move between tiles, Enter or Space to pick a tile, then Enter or Space on a neighbour to merge. On touch screens you can drag, or tap one tile and then the other.

I'm stuck – what does the Hint button do?

Hint highlights the next merge on a correct path. If your current position can no longer be finished, it takes you back to your last good position first and then shows the next merge, so you never have to restart from scratch.
