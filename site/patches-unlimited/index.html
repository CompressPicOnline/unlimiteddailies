How to play Patches

  1. Draw a patch. Press on a square and drag to the opposite corner of the rectangle you want, then let go. The dashed preview shows its size while you drag.
  2. One clue per patch. Every patch must contain exactly one clue – never zero, never two.
  3. Match the number. The number on a clue is the patch's area. A 6 can be 1×6, 2×3, 3×2 or 6×1 – as long as it fits the shape.
  4. Match the shape. square: equal width and height. wide: wider than it is tall. tall: taller than it is wide. dotted oval: any rectangle, squares included.
  5. Cover everything. The puzzle is solved when every square belongs to exactly one correct patch. Patches that break a rule are striped in red at once.

Patches strategy: six habits that solve most boards

1. Start with the clue that has the fewest options

Before drawing anything, look for a clue that can only be placed one way. A square clue with the number 4 has just one outline, 2×2, and next to a wall or in a corner it often has only one position. A 1 is always a single square. Place these first; every placed patch removes options from its neighbours.

2. Use the edges and corners

A clue in a corner can only grow away from the corner. A tall clue on the top edge can only extend downwards. Edges cut the number of possible rectangles roughly in half, which is why experienced players work from the outside in.

3. Look for squares no one else can reach

Every square must belong to some patch. If an empty square can be reached by only one clue – because every other clue is too small, too far away or the wrong shape – that clue's patch must include it. This single idea solves most of the harder boards.

4. Count what is left

When a region of the board is fenced off by finished patches, the numbers inside it must add up to its size. If a region of nine squares holds a 4 and a clue without a number, the numberless patch must be exactly 5 squares.

5. Let wide and tall clues guide you

A wide clue with the number 6 can only be 1×6 or 2×3, and a tall 6 only 6×1 or 3×2. On a 6×6 board a wide 6 is very often a full row. Shape icons are as much information as numbers – read both.

6. Leave the "any shape" clues for later

The dotted "any shape" clue allows every rectangle, including a square. It is the least informative clue, so leave it until its neighbours are placed and the space around it has shrunk.

Common Patches mistakes

  • Swallowing a second clue. A rectangle that looks perfect but touches another clue is never correct. Check the edges of every patch you draw.
  • Mixing up wide and tall. Wide means the patch is longer from left to right; tall means it is longer from top to bottom. A square is neither.
  • Leaving orphan squares. After each patch, glance at the empty squares next to it. If one of them can no longer be reached by any clue, the patch you just drew is wrong.
  • Guessing on big numbers. Large numbers have many possible outlines. Place the small, fixed clues around them first and the big patch usually has only one shape left.

Worked example: a corner and a strip

Picture the top row of a 5×5 board. In the top-left corner there is a clue showing 2 with the wide shape, and two squares to its right, in the third column, a clue showing 3 with the wide shape. The 2 can only be 1×2, and from the corner it can only grow to the right – so it covers columns 1 and 2. The 3 is wide too, so it is 1×3; it cannot reach left any more, so it covers columns 3, 4 and 5. Two clues, two forced patches, and the whole top row is finished without trying a single alternative. The second row now starts with a clean edge above it, which usually makes the next clue just as easy.

What do the number and the little shape on a clue mean?

The number is the area of the patch – how many squares it covers. The shape tells you its outline: a solid square means width and height are equal, a wide box means wider than tall, a tall box means taller than wide, and a dotted oval means any rectangle is allowed.

What does a clue with a question mark mean?

On Hard, Expert and some Daily boards a few clues show only a shape and no number. The patch must still contain that clue and have that shape, but you work out its size from the clues around it.

Does every Patches board have exactly one answer?

Yes. Before a board is shown, our solver searches for every way to split it. If it finds more than one, the generator makes a clue stricter and checks again, so every board has exactly one solution that you can reach by logic.

How do I draw and remove a patch?

Press on a square and drag to the opposite corner, then let go. A new patch replaces any patch it overlaps. Tap a patch once to remove it, or use Undo. Tapping an empty square creates a one-square patch.

What does the Hint button do?

If one of your patches is not part of the answer, Hint removes it. Otherwise it places the patch for the clue with the fewest possible positions left and tells you how many options that clue had.

Can I play with a keyboard?

Yes. Move with the arrow keys, press Enter or Space to start a patch, stretch it with the arrows and press Enter again to place it. Escape cancels and Delete removes the patch under the cursor.

Do hints or undo affect my stats?

Undo never affects anything. Hints are mentioned in the result you share, but your solved count, best times and daily streak still go up.

Is there a Patches solver?

Yes. If you are stuck on any board, open our Patches solver, enter each clue with its number and shape, and reveal the answer one patch at a time.

Is my progress saved if I close the page?

Yes. The patches you have drawn, your hints and your time are saved in this browser as you play, so you can come back to the same board later and carry on. Restart clears the saved board.
