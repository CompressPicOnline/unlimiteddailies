How to play Queens

  1. One queen per row and column. Every horizontal row and every vertical column ends up with exactly one queen.
  2. One queen per colour. The board is split into coloured regions, and each region gets exactly one queen too.
  3. Queens never touch. No queen may sit next to another one – not beside it, above it, below it or diagonally on any of the eight surrounding squares.
  4. Mark what you rule out. Tap once to put ✕ on a square that cannot hold a queen, tap twice to place a queen. Drag across squares to cross out a whole row or region quickly.
  5. Finish the board. When every row, column and colour has its queen and nothing clashes, the puzzle is solved. Clashing queens turn red so you notice mistakes at once.

Queens strategy: five techniques that solve most boards

1. Start with the smallest region

A region with only two or three squares leaves very few options. If all its squares sit in one row, the queen of that row must be inside that region – so you can cross out every other square of the row.

2. Regions locked into a line

The same idea works for columns, and for groups: if two regions fit entirely inside the same two rows, those two rows are "used up" by them. Every other square in those two rows can be marked with ✕.

3. Cross out the neighbours of every queen

As soon as you place a queen, mark its row, its column, its region and all eight squares around it. Our drag-to-mark makes this fast. Many boards then fall like dominoes.

4. Test a square by its consequences

When a square looks possible, imagine a queen there and see what it would cover. If it would wipe out every remaining square of some other region, that square must be ✕. This "what would it block?" check is the key to Hard and Expert boards.

5. Count what is left

Near the end, count open rows, columns and regions. If one region has squares in only one open column, its queen is forced. Counting is also a quick way to notice a mistake before the board turns red.

Worked example: a two-square region

Suppose a small yellow region covers just two squares, side by side in row 3. Whichever of the two holds the queen, row 3 is now spoken for, so every other square in row 3 gets an ✕. And because the two squares are neighbours, the squares directly above and below both of them touch the queen either way – so those can be crossed out too. One tiny region has just removed up to a dozen squares, without placing a single queen.

What are the rules of Queens?

Place exactly one queen in each row, each column and each coloured region. Queens may not touch each other – not side by side and not diagonally. The puzzle is solved when the board holds one queen per row, column and colour.

Yes. Each board is checked by our solver before you see it, and it is only used if exactly one arrangement of queens works. That means you can always solve it by logic, without guessing.

What are the ✕ marks for?

Tap a square once to mark it with ✕ when you know a queen cannot go there. Tap again to place a queen, and a third time to clear the square. You can also drag across several squares to mark them all with ✕ in one go.

What is the difference between the levels?

Easy is a 6×6 board, Medium 7×7, Hard 9×9 and Expert a big 10×10 board. The daily puzzle is 8×8. Bigger boards have more regions and longer chains of deductions.

What does the Hint button do?

If you have a queen or ✕ in the wrong place, Hint clears that mistake first. Otherwise it places one correct queen in the smallest unsolved region, which is usually where a human solver would look next.

What does Auto ✕ do?

When Auto ✕ is switched on, placing a queen automatically crosses out its row, its column, its colour region and the eight squares around it. It saves taps on big boards; switch it off if you prefer to do every deduction yourself.

Do hints or undo affect my stats?

Undo never affects anything. Hints are counted only in the result you share, so you can be honest with friends; your solved count and streak still go up.
